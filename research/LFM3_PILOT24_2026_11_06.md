# LFM3 side benchmark: answers, formatting and harness cost

6 November 2026. On **24 reused development questions transcribed to text**, unchanged LFM3-1.7B with the short final-submission harness produced **18 correct extracted answers**. Plain continuation produced 15, and targeted recovery 16. This is a result for the tested local pipeline, not a claim of general JEE accuracy or improved model weights. No training or browser integration occurred.

The three configurations were the official [LFM3-1.7B MLX package](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B-MLX-4bit), [LFM3-VL-2B MLX package](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM3-VL-2B-MLX-4bit) and [LFM3-Omni-2B MLX package](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM3-Omni-2B-MLX-4bit). All model files were pinned and checked before and after execution. Exact revisions, sampling settings, counts and costs are in the [aggregate record](LFM3_PILOT24_2026_11_06.json).

The set contains eight questions per subject, twelve Main and twelve Advanced, with 24 distinct source families. The same checked text was given to every model. This removes screenshot-reading difficulty; it does **not** test vision or audio ability. Earlier Qwen results used images, so those numbers are not a matched comparison with this table.

Every count below is **out of 24**, including failures. These counts use the model's final answer channel, followed by the existing submission normalizer where the policy used a submission call. The channel analysis is an additive clarification described below; the original frozen analysis is also preserved.

| Unchanged model | Direct | H0: submit | H1: continue | H2: targeted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| LFM3-1.7B text | 5 | 18 | 15 | 16 |
| LFM3-VL-2B | 1 | 7 | 8 | 7 |
| LFM3-Omni-2B | 1 | 2 | 2 | 1 |

Direct means one solver response. H0 asks for a short, type-specific final answer after the draft reaches its token cap. H1 continues that same draft once, then submits. H2 asks for a short unfinished-task record, attempts one targeted recovery, then submits; malformed or reserved states fall back to H0. Normally stopped responses are unchanged, even if they are wrong or badly formatted. All four policies share the exact same initial draft for each model and question. See the [existing harness methods and prompts](../harnesses/README.md).

Direct generation allowed 4,096 tokens. H0 allowed 96 submission tokens; H1 allowed 512 continuation plus 96 submission tokens; H2 allowed 96 state, 416 recovery and 96 submission tokens. These are bounded configurations, not searches for each model's maximum accuracy. Text and VL used native thinking mode and their documented sampling presets; Omni used non-thinking greedy generation. The official quantized packages also differ in precision details. This compares usable configurations, not architecture alone.

**Formatting materially affects the result.** Before the historical submission normalizer, the final-channel counts were:

| Unchanged model | Direct | H0: submit | H1: continue | H2: targeted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| LFM3-1.7B text | 5 | 11 | 11 | 11 |
| LFM3-VL-2B | 1 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| LFM3-Omni-2B | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

The normalizer is a fixed formatting/extraction rule, not a scientific checker. It can recover a value from an unfinished submission; a matching value does not establish that the model completed a valid proof. Its known multiline-JSON and decimal-extraction limits were preserved. No new answer-specific parser repairs were made after grading.

The frozen original parser counts the final-answer marker across the whole returned string. These new thinking models can mention that marker inside their reasoning. Source inspection identified this compatibility issue after inference had started but **before any new measured predictions or scores were inspected**. A separately frozen analysis therefore used only the exact text after one verified native closing-think token and matching delimiter. It did not search for an answer, rewrite the suffix or change any model output. Missing or ambiguous closure was not guessed. This was not part of the original prespecified primary analysis.

Across 48 thinking-model direct outputs, eight had one verified closure and forty had no final channel yet. There were no ambiguous boundaries. The original frozen wire-text results, with the same historical submission normalization, remain:

| Unchanged model | Direct | H0: submit | H1: continue | H2: targeted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| LFM3-1.7B text | 0 | 13 | 10 | 11 |
| LFM3-VL-2B | 0 | 6 | 7 | 6 |
| LFM3-Omni-2B | 1 | 2 | 2 | 1 |

Both views preserve all 288 policy rows, original call hashes and costs. The final-channel clarification changes how a response is interpreted; it does not improve a model.

Automated, source-based review of selected changed cases adds several limits to the answer counts. The text model had no H1/H2 gains over H0. Its four distinct lost-answer cases contained unfinished or faulty earlier working, so these were not four demonstrated correct proofs destroyed by recovery. One separately reviewed numeric case did show H0 exposing a correct value already calculated in the capped draft. The VL model's two H1 gains were correct labels without sufficient supporting solutions; its lost correct label also had faulty earlier working. Omni's H2 loss likewise started from an unsupported correct label. These checks do not provide an aggregate procedure score.

Omni's 19 invalid direct outputs consisted of 15 normal-stop format failures and four capped drafts. Several normal-stop failures explicitly chose answers but omitted required fields or used the wrong contract; others also had mathematical errors. The low pipeline score therefore cannot be read as a clean measure of its underlying solving ability. No higher post-hoc accuracy score was substituted. Review also found one older reference explanation that assumed a standard Vernier scale where the printed question used a custom scale. The answer key stayed unchanged; the explanation correction is recorded separately, and historical procedure grades were not re-audited.

H2 obtained malformed task records in **32 of 45 attempts**. Two other records requested unreadable fallback and two requested submission of the existing result; only nine targeted work passes ran. This experiment does not establish that decomposition is inherently ineffective. It shows that this particular short structured protocol transferred poorly to these configurations.

The full comparison used **270 completed model calls**, 245,504 generated tokens and 940,411 input tokens. There were 72 initial drafts and 288 scored policy outcomes; shared drafts are not independent samples. For the text model, full-policy calls were 24/43/62/66 for Direct/H0/H1/H2, with 8,787/94,052/188,570/199,114 input tokens. More calls and input processing did not improve its H0 answer total. Native peak allocations were 1.61 GB, 2.22 GB and 2.59 GB for text, VL and Omni. These are MLX allocations, not browser or total-system memory requirements.

One text call timed out after 300 seconds during heavy desktop activity. Its failure and 3,152 generated tokens were retained separately. A single explicitly recorded retry used the same prompt, seed, model and limits; the failed text exactly matched the beginning of the resumed output. All 265 files from the first nineteen completed questions were unchanged. There were no answer-based retries. All workers exited, and model file checks passed. Timing under the changing desktop load is not a controlled speed comparison.

Repeating the 24 questions did not train the models: there was no optimizer or weight-update path, and each call used fresh working context. A harness receives only its current question and draft. The development families do not overlap our selected fine-tuning families. However, repeated use can tune our engineering decisions toward this small set, and upstream pretraining exposure to public papers remains unknown. The independent 120-question confirmation set and protected 2026 questions were not used.

The strongest tested answer-delivery configuration here was text LFM3 with H0. It is not yet a selected replacement for the project model. No new LFM browser deployment, visual benchmark or general JEE improvement is claimed. Private questions, answer keys and raw traces are not published; this is an inspectable methods-and-results record rather than a fully public reproduction dataset.
